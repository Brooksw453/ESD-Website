## What I'm seeing this week

Here's a pattern I keep running into, and it's a frustrating one because everyone involved is doing their job well.

An accessibility lead spends a semester clearing a document backlog. Real progress — thousands of files fixed, a process that works. Then I ask what got purchased that same semester, and there's a pause. A new student-facing platform. A new form system. A new LMS plugin. None of it ran past anyone for accessibility before the contract was signed.

The remediation budget and the procurement process don't talk to each other. So the institution is mopping the floor with the tap still running. You can fix documents faster than anyone and still fall behind, because every new inaccessible tool you sign is next year's remediation project — except now it's contractually locked in for three years.

I saw this most clearly with a New England state system that had a genuinely strong remediation effort underway while, in a different building, signing a system-wide contract nobody had reviewed. Two good teams, no connection between them.

The fix here is not more remediation. You cannot clean your way out of a procurement problem. The fix is a gate: nothing student- or staff-facing gets signed without an accessibility check first. It is the cheapest thing on this list and the one almost nobody has in place.

## One thing you can do in five minutes

Send one email to whoever owns procurement intake. Ask for one rule: before any student- or staff-facing software is purchased, the vendor must provide a current accessibility conformance report (a VPAT/ACR) dated within the last twelve months, and a named person has to read it before sign-off.

You are not asking them to become accessibility experts. You are asking for one document and one named reviewer. That single sentence in a procurement process stops more future work than a quarter of remediation does.

## Worth your time

[Section508.gov](https://www.section508.gov/) — the US government's accessibility hub. It is not higher-ed specific, but its procurement and "how to read a conformance report" material translates directly to what you need when a vendor hands you a VPAT. Free, plain-language, no signup.
