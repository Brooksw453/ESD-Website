Who this course is for

Every role in higher ed that touches a document, a slide deck, or a quiz has a stake in Title II compliance. This course meets each of them where they are.

📚

Faculty & instructors

Tenure-track, adjunct, or clinical — anyone whose name is on a syllabus. Learn to build materials your students can actually use, without a technical background.

🎯

Instructional designers

The people building courses alongside faculty. Get a shared vocabulary and concrete checklists for reviewing accessibility before a course goes live.

🏫

CTL & faculty development

Teaching & Learning Center staff leading accessibility training. Use this course as the backbone of your institution's compliance rollout. Site licenses available.

What's inside

Eight modules across 6–8 hours, each self-paced with listen-and-speak lessons, AI-guided support, concrete examples, and a practical checklist you'll use the next week.

01

Why accessibility matters in higher ed

Title II in plain English. What the deadline actually means for you. Real student stories that stick.

02

WCAG 2.2 without the jargon

The 13 new criteria introduced in 2.2, what they mean for faculty work, and how to spot the ones that apply to your materials.

03

Accessible documents (Word, PDF, PowerPoint)

Headings, reading order, alt text, tables, links. The five things that fix 80% of document accessibility problems.

04

Accessible course materials

Syllabi, handouts, slides, readings, infographics. Examples from real college courses in STEM, humanities, and social sciences.

05

Accessible multimedia

Captions, transcripts, audio descriptions, and when each one is required vs. recommended. Tools faculty can actually use.

06

Accessible assessments and quizzes

Building quizzes that work with screen readers, extended-time accommodations, and keyboard-only navigation. Includes Canvas and Blackboard specifics.

07

Working with Disability Services

How to respond to accommodation letters, build proactive relationships with your DS office, and avoid the most common accessibility-related grievances.

08

Tools and workflows

Microsoft Accessibility Checker, Canvas Ally, Document Ally Pro, and free tools. A personal workflow you can adopt this semester.

By the end of the course, you will

  • Audit your own syllabus against WCAG 2.2 AA in under ten minutes
  • Fix the five most common document accessibility issues without technical help
  • Build quizzes and assessments that work for every student on day one
  • Explain Title II compliance to a colleague in one minute flat
  • Know when to escalate to Disability Services vs. handle a request yourself
  • Earn a completion certificate suitable for professional development records
